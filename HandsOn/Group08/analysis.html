Analysis of the data sources: Madrid sports facilities

Group 08 · Hands-on 2. This document contains (1) the analysis of the data, (2) the analysis of the licensing, and (3) the resource naming strategy. The datasets are the three CSV files in csv/, all published on the Madrid City Council open data portal.

1. Analysis of the data sets

1.1 Overview

FileDataset (portal)RowsColumnsSize
instalaciones_deportivas_basicas.csvDeportes. Instalaciones deportivas básicas municipales60732351 KB

Format: CSV, semicolon-separated, ISO-8859-1 (Latin-1) encoding, one header row. All three files have exactly the same 32 columns, because they come from the same generic structure the City Council uses for all datasets coming from madrid.es. In total there are 745 rows. The portal updates the datasets daily; our copies are the ones downloaded in October 2026.

Each row is one facility, with its own PK. The PK is unique in each file (607, 82 and 56 distinct values). The basic facilities are outdoor, single-sport spaces (football pitches, futsal courts, skating rinks, petanque courts, calisthenics areas...) and are usually free to use. The sports centres (polideportivos) are large multi-sport complexes. The swimming pools file lists municipal centres that have pools.

Observations on the schema:

  • Four columns (HORARIO, EQUIPAMIENTO, TRANSPORTE, DESCRIPCION) contain unstructured text, originally HTML with the tags removed. In the sports centres and pools the equipment text is long (about 815 characters on average in the centres file) and describes classes, surfaces and indoor and outdoor units. In the basic facilities it is a short phrase (about 58 characters), for example "Pista de fútbol sala. Acceso libre.".
  • PLANTA, ESCALERAS and FAX are empty in the basic facilities; PLANTA, PUERTA, ESCALERAS are empty or almost empty in all files. They carry no information for our application.
  • The address is split into street type (CLASE-VIAL), street name, number type and number. The postal code, neighbourhood and district are given as codes and names.
  • LOCALIDAD and PROVINCIA always contain "Madrid" (one centre has "Madrid" with different capitalisation and an empty province). They add no information per row.
  • Locations are given twice: UTM zone 30 (ETRS89) in COORDENADA-X/Y and WGS84 in LATITUD/LONGITUD. We will use the WGS84 pair for RDF.
  • The TIPO value is a path such as /contenido/entidadesYorganismos/CentrosEspaciosDeporte/PistaFutbolSala; only the last segment is meaningful.

1.5 Accessibility

The documentation defines ACCESIBILIDAD as a list of codes: 0 = not accessible, 1 = accessible, 2 = partially accessible for people with reduced mobility, 3 = no information on accessibility for people with reduced mobility, 4 = sign language, 5 = tactile paving, 6 = magnetic induction loop. Codes 0 to 3 describe mobility; codes 4 to 6 add features. In the data one cell can hold several codes separated by a comma, for example 1,5,6.

1.7 Relation between the files

  • Basic facilities are disjoint from the others: no PK in common with the sports centres or pools.
  • Pools and sports centres are largely the same entities: 55 of the 56 PK values in the pools file also appear in the sports centres file, and 38 of those rows are completely identical. The remaining 17 differ in some columns, mainly TIPO and the text fields. Only 1 pool, Club de Campo Villa de Madrid (PK 72028), is not in the sports centres file. In the RDF we will therefore model one individual per PK and mark the centres that have a pool, instead of creating two individuals for the same place.

2. Analysis of the licensing

PublisherAyuntamiento de Madrid, through its open data portal datos.madrid.es. The responsible unit shown on the dataset pages is the Dirección General de Deporte.
Rights holderAyuntamiento de Madrid. The portal does not name a different rights holder: the data comes from the municipal website madrid.es and is published by the City Council itself under its open licence. We therefore take the Ayuntamiento de Madrid as rights holder.

Licence for the generated dataset

We will publish the RDF dataset and the ontology under CC BY 4.0. Reasons:

  • It is the same licence as the source, so the requirement to attribute the City Council is passed on to everybody who reuses our data, and the source terms are not made stricter or looser than needed.
  • It allows republishing, linking and commercial reuse, which fits an open linked dataset.
  • The transformation to RDF, the URL minting, the cleaning described in 1.8 and the links to other datasets are changes. The licence requires us to say so; the dataset description will state that it is derived from the Ayuntamiento de Madrid datasets and has been modified.

The licence will be declared in the data with dcterms:license <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/> and the source with dcterms:source pointing to each original dataset page. A short attribution text will be included in the dataset description: "Contains data of the Ayuntamiento de Madrid (datos.madrid.es), licensed under CC BY 4.0. Transformed to RDF by Group 08."
